## Policy amendment, ready for review

[proposed/agents-policy.patch](proposed/agents-policy.patch) is a **reference-only proposed patch** against this exact main. It changes AGENTS and its actual owning policy tests, not any checked-out product. The proposed guide has **2,053 words**, within the unchanged **2,100-word** limit. The original file checksum was verified; proposed Python syntax was compiled; no product tests were run.

The patch covers all five conflicting areas, not only the obvious performance heading:

1. Bounded execution does not mean persisting every scheduler yield. An operation owns its interruption/recovery contract.
2. Growth bounds remain blocking; approved production budgets become a separate contractual acceptance dimension; ordinary validation-runtime observations remain advisory.
3. Replace “Measure performance without creating gates” with “Enforce production performance budgets”: raw elapsed wall time; approved complete workload/profile; independent authority outside candidate source; three complete correct observations, each within budget; invalid/incomplete/unqualified/miss cannot pass.
4. Keep automatic validation timing once-per-execution, target-free measurements, diagnostic comparisons, and no approval condition for ordinary CI/test runtime misses. Required production-budget failure is separately propagated.
5. Final handoff reports correctness, growth, required budget verdicts and timing observations without misclassifying a required budget miss as merely a recommendation.

Durability guidance chooses the simplest project-owned design meeting declared promises and budgets, without a universal replay target, checkpoint fraction or persistence-on-yield mandate. It preserves existing protected commitments and interruption/retry/custody proof. It does not authorize losing confirmed outcomes, removing independent recovery, discarding durable user state, or weakening security/growth.

The patch links the immutable shared performance contract. The acceptance implementation still must be integrated; updating guide prose and its hash alone is not completion.

Current guide rules also require preserving issue bodies, recording later decisions in comments, and using a linked successor rather than reopening/rewriting completed work. [#963](https://github.com/nashspence/riverhog/issues/963) is closed/completed and explicitly established advisory targets. Supersede its conflicting production-timing authority prospectively in a comment and new adoption issue. Apply the same history preservation to completed #964. Keep issue/PR tracking out of main source.

## Governance, completion and cost control

Main `release.toml` explicitly describes an active no-bypass linear-history/force-push/deletion ruleset **without PR or pre-push status gate**. The observed branch endpoint reports protected=true but legacy required status contexts empty/off. Meanwhile CI has a “CI gate” and exact-source post-push validation policy. These are not proof of independent protected performance acceptance. Integrator must reconcile the chosen dispatch/protection/approval rail with release governance and live rulesets; the new issue must not assert it already exists. Keep provider and publication permissions separate.

The shared source requirement is stronger than Riverhog's current helper: it includes ignored files. An apparently clean main checkout containing .venv/pycache/generated builds may be rejected; isolated admitted source plus external artifacts/work roots avoids weakening either check. Source pins must identify actually invoked built/installed software, not merely a nearby source commit.

Validation should stay purposeful: focused policy/adapter/affected-owner tests during implementation, one exact-source integrated CI qualification at handoff, and only the common three observations per approved bounded operation. No arbitrary rerun-until-green, no dropping slow valid observations, no empty/hidden workload, no 1000/10000/100000 escalation before the small path has a concrete disposition. Large qualification remains separately scoped and approved as required by its owner. A completed budget miss is evidence to fix or propose a reviewed contract change; an incomplete watchdog timeout is failed, never a fast sample.

Acceptance for the adoption slice requires the guide/test migration, coherent executable ownership; explicit retirement of #994 and #995–#1000; a preserved #1001 checkpoint and completed finite salvage disposition; any configuration/state changes required by the selected new design; approved authority/profile/binding, real first-operation evidence with common verdict propagation, issue-surface supersession preserving history, and exact-source correctness/growth/receiving proof. Other family budgets may continue as explicitly owned linked work; do not claim campaign completion while they are undisposed.
