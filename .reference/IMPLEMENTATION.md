# Collection publication with minimal durable effects

This is the selected **proposal** for [#997](https://github.com/nashspence/riverhog/issues/997), under [#994](https://github.com/nashspence/riverhog/issues/994). It is concrete integration input, not an accepted product contract or completed implementation. Start at [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md) for provenance and source identity, [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) for exact effects and failure handling, and [QUALIFICATION.md](QUALIFICATION.md) for the qualification sequence. The product tree is unchanged from `c28eceaa2dfec1b3712135469ba12230e74f5ffa`.

## 1. The implementation decision

Use one bounded, live publication owner per construction. Its mandatory records preserve original inputs and effects; its private conductor runs registration planning, Native validation, history construction and index preparation without a durable Core update for each execution yield. It adopts a replay frontier only after a grant from the shared #995 policy and a complete #996 generation. It adopts required semantic outcomes and final publication regardless of checkpoint eligibility.

Reuse the correct existing receiving, effect, prepared-primary and catalog-generation mechanisms. Refactor their granularity and preparation/acceptance split. Do not implement a new universal transaction framework, a second fast executor, a fixed number of durable lifecycle states, or a blanket change to database synchronization.

Three kinds of writes must remain distinguishable in code and evidence:

| Write | Reason and selected treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Original intent, exact observation/preimage, random encryption continuation, accepted selection, verified result, custody or terminal receiving | Mandatory at the owner's actual external-effect/acknowledgement boundary. Independently identified records may share a bounded atomic transaction. |
| Required final archive/catalog/index representation | Install complete output in bounded batches under hidden generation identity. These bytes are needed by the final product even with optional recovery disabled. The transaction boundary is an admitted installation batch, not a Native semantic step. |
| Replay cursor, private validation stage, serialized conductor, resume selector, checkpoint infrastructure | Optional recovery work. Cheap execution creates none of its stores/generations. Only an admitted, consistent frontier may be selected as resumable. |

A row written during speculative construction does not become mandatory merely because it is called an index or receipt. Required representation installation begins from a coherent prepared output; extra progress copies or early durable snapshots are charged as optional. Existing mandatory sealed objects may be reused as verified replay inputs, but do not prove semantics that were never validated.

## 2. Inputs already settled by the adjacent references

Consume these exact, separately owned reference inputs after reconciliation with their issues:

Use `ArchiveWriteControlRecord` as the surviving collection budget root; `CollectionUploadRecord` is retired at publication. Keep policy/accounting outside replaceable `construction_json`. The root is initialized in the original construction transaction, survives worker epochs/retries/phases and is finalized only after required receiving and charged cleanup. SDK/Core/Native share its allowance; they do not get separate percentages.

Defaults are the #995 proposal: adaptive mode, 120 seconds of net saved replay, optional fraction 1/8, unlimited successful checkpoint count and two usable generations. Net saved replay already deducts predicted restore/revalidation. Do not charge that prediction again as failure-free creation cost. Credit useful root elapsed once, including overlapping children only once. Reserve cost/count before optional work; account failed attempts and attributable setup/encoding/coordination/contention/reclamation. Unknown outcome or unknown charge closes further optional admission while mandatory authorized semantics continue.

The Core durable selector is a typed `resume_frontier_json`, replacing mixed `progress_json`; it binds the complete generation, selected input closure and owner receipt. Native generation persistence precedes one fenced Core transaction selecting it and consuming the grant. A progress update never erases or advances this selector. Cheap semantic completion writes the necessary exact-source result, freezes the live representation for borrowed readers, and does not force a final optional snapshot. Cold derived-cache loss goes through the #996 coalesced bounded rebuild keyed by construction, exact terminal outcome, complete binding and algorithm. Missing source, receipt, outcome or authorization is never a cache miss.

Before a SDK operation is bound to an accepted Core construction, its necessary original preparation remains mandatory and it cannot spend or mint a separate Core optional allowance. After binding, optional SDK work requires a grant tied to that surviving root and exact logical task. Receipt reconciliation and required local byte custody do not depend on optional eligibility.

### Public activation is part of the coupled cut

At the audited source, [the public unit route](https://github.com/nashspence/riverhog/blob/c28eceaa2dfec1b3712135469ba12230e74f5ffa/riverhog/src/riverhog_api/routers/collections.py) still assembles `bytearray` content before calling legacy `collection_uploads.upload_unit`; public cancel/discard call legacy `.cancel`/`.discard_orphan`. The newer input reservation, exact-intent dispatch and terminal owners are private integration mechanisms. The passing unchanged F1 run does **not** establish that this private protocol is already active.

The first proposed public cut wires original input admission/reservation **before body assembly**, bounded accepted body ownership, the exact original actor and effect guards, all forward finalization writers, and tracked terminal execution together. Preserve the current `Content-Length`, `If-Match`, access and conflict semantics. Failed/disconnected body assembly releases only its positively untransferred reservation; submitted or uncertain originals remain owned. A route must not publish an asynchronous result whose matching terminal/recovery owner is still bypassed. Replace the old route path in the same integration; do not silently fall back to it on capacity, checkpoint ineligibility or cache loss. The shared activation seam follows the already accepted #984 contracts and the then-current #979 rail, with one Core implementation lead.

Use the prepared source seams: `_reserve_payload_input` before streaming, `ArchivePayloadInput.append/seal`, then `_submit_payload_input` through `ArchiveUploadInvocationRegistry` → `ArchivePayloadUnit` → `ArchiveUploadWrites`/`ExecutingArchiveResumableObjectStore`, with original receiving. Couple `cancel_collection_upload_session` and `discard_collection_upload` to `ArchiveWriteAdmission.select_terminal` and the original terminal registry/turn/result/readback owners. Keep `plan_collection_upload_discard` **read-only**: it returns the bound current challenge/frontier; it does not install terminal intent or close/freeze the live construction. Only the confirmed discard request selects the terminal action. In the service constructor, pass `ArchiveUploadTerminalDue` into `ArchiveUploadMaintenance`; the audited default `terminal_execution=None` leaves that work unwired despite creation of `_archive_terminals`. Enroll its shutdown/receiving in the existing lifecycle.

Preserve the current open-gate checks for create/resume, tags, member/raw registration, completion requirement/recording reservation, journal create/append/seal, primary/history/materialization binding, structure staging, complete and heartbeat. Every newly batched acceptance repeats the required current check. Keep finalization's existing original pipeline/claim coupling and due/startup admission guards. Never reconstruct a live dispatch capability from a durable row at startup.

## 4. Registration without durable planning microsteps

`CollectionRegistrationRecord` already retains the canonical request, partition of new members, base planner state and revision, construction identity, execution epoch and authorization fences. Those are the replay inputs and idempotent request identity. Keep them, the surviving acknowledgement, capacity errors and original disposal decision. A lost response must retrieve the same acknowledgement after upload retirement.

Move `stage_frontier_json`, `verify_frontier_json`, stage/verify chain and totals, and coverage-audit cursors into the private registration conductor. Preserve their checks as computations; delete their unconditional durable projection. Use existing `VolumeEmissionLimits` for each quantum and owner-private disk scratch for a plan larger than memory. Do not collect the whole archive in one tuple. The existing request-size contract limits one request, not the collection.

Prepare and verify the exact plan, including complete member-to-volume coverage, outside the shared writer. Install required plan/member/coverage rows in admitted batches tagged to the accepted operation. Until the final registration acknowledgement selects the complete plan, neither upload-unit acquisition nor another producer can treat provisional rows as accepted work. In the same short acceptance transaction, recheck `_live` equivalents (construction, base revision, tag selection, active operation, lease, current key/claim), select the verified planner outcome, update counts, clear the active registration and retain the exact acknowledgement. This is the meaning of registration completion; intermediate private phase count is not a contract.

If a plan-install batch loses its commit reply, use exact row keys and immutable content to receive that same batch; do not allocate different members or overwrite conflicting plan rows. With no selected optional frontier, reconstruct from the mandatory canonical request and base state; idempotently verify/reuse matching installed rows. An optional frontier can cover private plan state and its exact installed-prefix receipts only after the common grant. It cannot acknowledge a partial plan.

## 5. Finalization and catalog construction

### Preserve semantic dependency order

The current `_finalization_owner` already sequences Native sealing/original immutable report, final member histories, complete provenance closure, provenance structures, artifact tree, volume metadata, provenance volumes, provenance authority, collection authority, custody, initial tags/description and catalog projection. Its phase count may change. Its dependency obligations remain:

1. Accepted member IDs, primary journals/bindings, exact input-history selections and completion requirement are immutable inputs.
2. Record and accept exact execution-completion evidence, including the declared output/disposition preimages and original recording context. This evidence does not depend on the as-yet-unpublished final collection root.
3. Construct each final member-history descriptor from its accepted early primary, exact input selection and required completion-journal root. Verify full sets and counts; do not infer closure from a last cursor.
4. Validate all selected Native semantics and the complete canonical closure. Build the artifact set, provenance/volume structures and metadata from those exact selections; seal their encrypted objects through existing effect owners.
5. Seal immutable provenance/collection roots only when their dependency closure is complete. Early custody remains a narrower receipt; index failure must leave these sealed outcomes retained for recovery.
6. Complete required catalog/index representation and initial metadata obligations. Accept an exact publication candidate, then atomically make the complete catalog view and its complete index visible. Final receipt/settlement references the accepted completion and roots. Receive remaining original resources before reporting fully settled.

Implementation may interleave independent preparation and batch effects. It must not create a completion-journal → final-history → final-root → completion-journal cycle, skip index work because ciphertext is sealed, or silently drop an input still needed for an accepted completion obligation.

### Prepare outside the writer

Replace calls that perform complete replay/relevance/index extraction under `ArchiveCatalogFinalizer.advance()`'s writer with `prepare` work on detached immutable inputs. Keep the original prepared result and its resources owned until acceptance is known. A prepared batch is internally typed and binds construction, current execution claim, exact source/selection revisions, semantic/configuration/algorithm identity, output identities and bounded write set. It is not a public receipt.

The accepted source set closes once the relevant upload input selection is sealed. Add one operational `publication_input_revision` on the surviving control, advanced **within the same transaction** as every mutation of accepted member/evidence/history/completion selections it protects. Required derived-output installation has its own generation revision; it does not silently redefine accepted input. Capture a fixed-size binding of input revision, construction, accepted counts/selection roots and the selected immutable mandatory-receipt manifest/root identities. Do not assemble an unbounded receipt list inside the final transaction. Typed manifests/roots bind large dependency sets; bounded preparation verifies their complete closure. Any code permitted to change those inputs must participate in this revision protocol. A post-seal mutation either fails or invalidates the pending candidate. Authorization and terminal gates are checked afresh, not represented as permanently valid merely by this digest.

Read snapshots are bounded in rows/octet work and do not hold the shared writer. Capture one immutable build ID/worker epoch and exact input/content revisions at scan start. Read each bounded keyset page in a fresh source-consistent read snapshot that checks those **same starting** revisions; never adopt a later version mid-scan. Check revision and fetch the page within that snapshot, not in unrelated reads. Final acceptance uses current rows under owner locks in a fresh transaction, not stale ORM identity-map values. Immutable row keys, monotone generation-local versioning and complete selected extents prevent a mixed-version scan from being accepted. Versions are canonical nonnegative scalars and are never wrapped/reset/reused; rollback changes neither dataset nor revision. If a protected revision changes, abandon the derived candidate and start from a valid bound source; do not accept a stale prefix. Normal finalization freezes accepted inputs, so repeated rescans are not the successful-run algorithm.

### Required catalog row installation

Keep the existing unpublished collection and hidden index generation as the visibility boundary. Feed them already-prepared bounded row batches. Reuse complete source identities for idempotent insertion and conflicts; no provider I/O, Native replay, history traversal or full hash scan belongs inside an installation transaction. Each short transaction contains fresh construction/gate/claim checks, exact row mutations and their necessary receiving proof. It may span several small logical stages if the same resource envelope and fences cover them.

Output rows that the published catalog actually needs are required representation. Persisting a second cursor after every private step is not. A process death before final publication leaves either independently receivable immutable output batches or disposable/incomplete hidden work. Restart reuses only verified exact rows or a valid selected optional frontier; it never discovers a naked cursor and treats it as semantic authority.

### Index completion and final atomic publication

Current `complete_index_build()` checks snapshot/member/membership counts, per-snapshot entries/assertions, then hashes the full dataset. Preserve all these checks and the exact `_dataset_sha256`/generation encoding; **counts alone are insufficient**. Move the scan/hash into a bounded preparation owner using stable keyset pages and live count/hash accumulators. Do not replace the old writer-held scan with an uninterruptible whole-dataset call in each quantum. Add canonical nonnegative `content_revision` to the pending generation; every insert/update/delete of its dataset increments it in the same existing batch transaction. Cleanup, replacement and rebuild paths participate as well. Reject dataset mutations after complete, as the current owner already requires.

Mutation coverage includes all nine `_DATASET_TABLES`: snapshot, entry, assertion, profile, value, text chunk, edge, member and membership. Bulk/cascade cleanup and generation replacement must fence the candidate even if the parent or last visible row disappears. Do not reuse a build ID for another candidate. Tests must change profile/value/text/edge content without changing the three top-level counts, mutate between page-check and page-fetch boundaries, and replace the pending generation. Each stale proof must fail without losing sealed archive custody.

The private `PreparedIndexCompletion` contains build ID, expected worker epoch, construction and `publication_input_revision`, selected immutable source-binding digest, archive generation/root/provenance identity, core/extraction contract hashes, captured content revision, full validated counts, dataset hash and resulting generation ID. Accept it only under the existing collection/index-state lock when all bindings and the content revision still match. The acceptance transaction records the complete generation and exact completion result, including the input binding; it does not recompute the full dataset. Recheck that input binding again at final publication. A lost commit reply reconciles that exact result. A changed revision/epoch/root rejects the candidate. Corruption is not successful empty data.

For an initial collection, combine `publish_index_build()` and `_publish_catalog_collection()` in the same short final transaction. Recheck current construction, open gate, finalization claim and authorization; verify the candidate's mandatory dependencies and complete-generation seal; switch the index selector, set collection visibility, and emit the existing catalog/lifecycle events in that transaction. Store the immutable original `publication_receipt_json` and set `upload_retirement_pending` on the surviving control in that same transaction. This marker makes copy-intent activation eligible, without scanning/updating an unbounded list in the critical section. Required copy fan-out and retired upload-row deletion use bounded owned maintenance; delete the upload parent only after its children and original receivers are safely retired. Do not delete mandatory receipts, budget accounting or uncertain registration acknowledgements with that parent.

Select each published construction needing activation through the surviving pending marker, then page **only that construction's** accepted copy intents with the existing `(collection_id, state, destination_store)` index. In bounded transactions, change exact `accepted` intents to `pending` and set their due time; the existing pending due selector and `_handoff_upload_copy_intent` then retain their current shape. Complete this activation before clearing the retirement marker. Preserve handoff's fresh key/grant, source/destination incarnation and complete-source-copy checks: publication grants eligibility, not copy authority. Do not scan an unrelated global backlog of unpublished accepted intents. Copy intents already survive upload retirement; receiving publication does not wait for the separately owned copies to finish.

Use the current original publication tuple (`_expected_publication`/`_published_after`) extended to bind the initially selected index completion and input revision as the exact lost-reply proof. `publication_receipt_json` preserves that original seal after later legitimate derived-index rebuilds; it does not require the first `active_build_id` to remain selected forever. Parent-row absence, a Future result or any published collection with the same integer ID is insufficient. Final visibility does not imply that every original close/join has been received; expose result availability and lifecycle settlement separately.

### Exact operational schema additions

| Owner/field | Selected meaning and lifecycle |
| --- | --- |
| `ArchiveWriteControlRecord.publication_input_revision: Text` | Canonical nonnegative revision of accepted semantic inputs, initialized with construction; all protected input mutations advance it atomically. It survives upload retirement. |
| `ArchiveWriteControlRecord.publication_receipt_json: Text nullable` | Immutable mandatory original publication result: construction commitment, current publication tuple, input revision/source binding, initial complete index seal and event identity. Null until the atomic visibility transaction. Exact retries compare equality; no replacement on later index rebuild. |
| `ArchiveWriteControlRecord.upload_retirement_pending: Boolean` | Set with publication; cleared only by the original bounded retirement owner after its actual receiving/child retirement. Its existence grants no cleanup authority. |
| `CollectionProvenanceIndexGenerationRecord.content_revision: Text` | Canonical nonnegative monotone dataset mutation revision. Every stage/replacement/cleanup mutation updates it in the owning transaction; the completion seal binds its selected value. |
| Index completion's retained input-binding fields | Exact construction/input revision/source-binding digest on the completed generation, alongside existing dataset/generation/contract/root identities, so final publication can reject a stale semantic candidate. |

Forward-work due queries and admission reject a control with an existing publication receipt, even while upload rows await retirement. Public status prioritizes the retained publication receipt and published collection, not the leftover upload's old phase. Original result receiving, terminal readback and bounded retirement remain discoverable from the surviving control after publication and parent deletion. Their scheduling must not inherit the forward-work exclusion. Terminal mutation of an already published construction returns the existing final/declared refusal semantics rather than invoking legacy abort/delete. All these fields are operational state; none changes Native or archive identities or makes the database a substitute for archive authority.

This parent-retention change requires two explicit receiving adaptations. `ArchiveCatalogFinalizer` must capture its exact publication postimage/receipt **before commit whenever publication is selected**, rather than only when `inspect(upload).deleted` is true. On original retry, prefer that retained publication proof even if the upload row still exists. Registry/workflow feedback consumes the original published result and schedules owned retirement; `_requeue_finalization_step` must not enqueue fresh forward work or require a forward gate for that already published result.

Likewise, `ArchiveArtifactCustodyReceiptSource.apply()` must route an original published receipt to exact published readback when the control's publication receipt exists, rather than waiting for parent absence before `_verify_published`. The immutable final catalog member receipt supplies its exact acknowledgement. Pin every upload child, volume/checkpoint/effect record, source and result resource still needed by an original until that owner's receiving completes, or implement that owner's exact retained published readback before removing the dependency. The retirement marker alone authorizes no child deletion. Unknown originals remain retained; bounded deletion never turns their missing row into successful receiving.

## 6. Public progress and bounded resources

Hard-cut the relevant protocol/API, SDK and CLI status consumers together. Return required stable public states/receipts and two explicit progress views:

| Field group | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `processed` | Advisory counts of currently processed/validated work with a live execution identity. It can disappear or move backward after restart. No custody or replay guarantee. |
| `resumable` | Nullable exact selected frontier identity and covered counters, from the durable owner selector only. Null means no optional frontier; mandatory effects/receipts may still be retained. |
| outcome/custody/settlement | Existing exact result and early/final distinctions plus actual original receiving state; never inferred from either progress percentage. |

Use existing canonical scalar/count types and contracts for numeric fields. The external shape is defined with its protocol owner, generated schemas and all maintained consumers in one cut; no old field alias and no hidden per-step fallback. Keep transport offsets required to resume an actual provider effect in their existing transport contract: they are not renamed into optional semantic progress.

A status call reads bounded current control plus the last coalesced live sample. It performs no checkpoint, payload read, journal replay, provider wait or synchronization. The worker publishes live samples at existing quantum/yield boundaries, with bounded metadata; polling does not drive execution. Existing configured request/worker deadlines and cancellation remain. Fresh permission checks apply to every public request.

Use existing `JournalValidationBudget`, `VolumeEmissionLimits`, archive-write control/result capacity, SQLite admission and original retained-resource grants. Separate execution quantum limits from optional checkpoint policy. No collection-size cap is introduced. Every proposed batch is limited by rows, encoded octets, prepared-result capacity and cooperative work/deadline budget; stop before admitting the next unit that would exceed a dimension. One item that cannot fit returns the existing explicit capacity outcome. Do not split an indivisible original identity to fit, create an unbounded waiting queue, or hold unrelated constructions behind a global group-commit timer.

## 7. State cut and implementation order

The #995/#996/#997 first vertical slice is one coordinated change on the current integration rail. It is not three independent percentage policies or an alternate executor left indefinitely beside the old one.

1. Add the shared policy root and complete Native generation/outcome seam; wire the existing real F1 producer and finalizer through it together with the coupled public input/effect/terminal cut above. Remove per-Native-yield Core adoption. Preserve all original effects, encrypted storage, current authorization and original receiving. Demonstrate zero optional store creation in cheap restart/adaptive execution.
2. Apply the existing safe result batches and prepared-primary reuse; split registration and final catalog/index prepare/accept as above. Run the exact original-evidence, lost-reply, terminal and index witnesses as each owner changes. Required representation installation remains bounded.
3. On the **same path**, demonstrate default-eligible long/unknown replay work, complete generation selection, actual process restart and exact source/result receiving. Apply optional policy to the remaining derived delegates, with one root and no duplicated credits. Do not tune a default using the held #991 campaigns.
4. Carry state/configuration/protocol/client/CLI/growth/installed changes and qualify the final source under existing gates. Record the scoped #997 completion separately from parent completion and permission to resume #991.

For the current pre-v1 schema, replace superseded optional cursor/phase fields rather than accepting both formats. Preserve original intent, sealed receipt, random crypto continuation, registration acknowledgement, terminal challenge and operator decisions. The owning explicit upgrade/initialization path remains; startup only validates. Do not write a silent migration/reset of incompatible live operational state.

The selected operational disposition is: **quiesce/drain what the owning old executable can safely finish, retain the old state, and refuse opening or upgrading any populated incompatible pre-v1 layout with the new executable. Initialize the new baseline only in genuinely new/empty state.** Draining does not itself make the old schema compatible. Current `state-schema` only applies declared supported upgrades; a reused `v1_0001` with mismatching physical layout fails verification and an unknown revision refuses. The integration must test and expose that refusal, preserve all existing records with their old owner, and avoid inventing an export/transfer command or deleting records to get an empty database.

Any ambiguous original remains retained, with the old executable and its receiving obligations. There is no new-runtime reader for abandoned per-step formats, no automatic deletion, and no replay-from-payload substitute for original evidence. A separately reviewed, lossless owner transfer would require its own explicit implementation and authority; none is supplied or presumed here. Existing archived bytes/keys and supported future upgrade machinery remain intact. This is a concrete refusal/initialization policy, not an instruction for the integration agent to design an ad hoc migration during qualification.

## 8. Research basis and evidence limits

The proposal follows the repository's explicit obligations, not a generic checkpoint pattern. Stable caller intent and parameter matching are the relevant retry mechanism in [Amazon's idempotent API analysis](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-APIs/); source payload equality cannot replace a random original journal identity. [SQLite's synchronization semantics](https://www.sqlite.org/pragma.html#pragma_synchronous) and [PostgreSQL's asynchronous-commit documentation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/wal-async-commit.html) distinguish logical transactions from physical durability; neither permits acknowledging a mandatory effect from an unqualified asynchronous write. [Pillai et al.](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi14/technical-sessions/presentation/pillai) motivate platform/filesystem crash qualification, not a claim that Python `fsync`/rename observations prove power-loss survival.

The unchanged maintained F1 baseline and focused SDK/Core experiments are reported separately in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md). They establish current semantics and particular batching/receiving witnesses. They do not implement this complete conductor, qualify the proposed state cut or establish new-path F1 speedup, optional budget conformance, physical sync counts, provider behavior, installed offline recovery or #997 acceptance. The exact outstanding commands and acceptance witnesses are specified in QUALIFICATION; no unresolved architecture choice is delegated to the integration agent.
