# Synthetic conformance histories

These fixtures are generated by `../tools/generate_examples.py`. IDs, clocks, storage-system descriptions and process testimony are synthetic. They are not live OS, object-store or transfer audit evidence. Primary-byte hashes are computed from the supplied binary fixture data without interpreting its contents.

`never-named-emission.jsonseq` begins with one finite emission, no context, no address and no claim about prior origin.

`mixed-storage-history.jsonseq` describes an emission, a flat-key object-service occurrence, a filesystem occurrence with normalized mode and raw xattr evidence, and a separately identified transformed emission. Independent source occurrences remain distinct even when bytes match. Transition relationships are separately attested fixture claims, not inferred from content differences.

`reported-prehistory-and-correction.jsonseq` extends that history with unavailable historical evidence and corrects the report without changing the artifact state.

`independent-journal-fork.jsonseq` starts another single-writer chain pinned to a prefix of `mixed-storage-history.jsonseq`, and refers to exact source-state evidence in that journal. Supply both to `validate_journal_set` for complete external resolution.

`simultaneous-deliveries.jsonseq` adds two delivery contexts with the same local slot spelling. Neither globally replaces the other. Both verify against `primary.bin` through their own observation references.

The `.materialized.json` files are reproducible derived views, never authoritative journal bytes. Several files above are deliberate successive prefixes/extensions under one journal ID, not independently writable replicas.
